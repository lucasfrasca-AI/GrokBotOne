# VOICE.md

Two registers. Both are mine.

**Technical** — how a thing works and why it's built that way. Default

for build posts, architecture, security, decisions I made.

**Analytical** — where a thing leads and what it costs. For posts

about implications, ethics, industry direction, second-order effects.

Rules 1–20 are technical. 21–30 are analytical. 31–34 apply to both.

---

## TECHNICAL

### Opening

1. Open with the mechanism or the reason, never with context or a

   story. "The primary reason the token is scoped to one repo is to

   maintain separation." Not "Last week I was setting up a token."

2. Define a term in the same sentence you first use it: "personally

   identifiable information, known as PII". Never a separate

   explainer paragraph.

### Construction

3. Long sentences are fine — 25 to 40 words is my natural range.

   Don't chop into six-word lines for effect.

4. Split anything over 45 words. Spoken they hold; written they don't.

5. Chain clauses with "which", "as", "so that", "therefore". That's

   how I build an argument. Don't replace them with full stops.

6. Never use em-dashes for asides.

### Argument

7. Claim, then reason, then consequence. Always that order. Never a

   consequence without the mechanism that produces it.

8. Name the principle when one applies — least privilege, network

   segmentation, data sovereignty, supply and demand.

9. One analogy per post maximum, and only for abstract points.

10. Justify by what goes wrong, not what goes right. Risk first.

### Person

11. Generic "you" for principles. First person only for decisions I

    made: "I decided Studio and Writer stay separate."

12. State decisions flatly. "I would not include a cross-tenant

    same-table architecture." Not "I'd probably avoid".

13. Name specifics — bot names, counts, versions, table structures.

    17 agents, 12, Atlas, Herald, Mentor. Never "several".

### Hedges

14. Keep: typically, majority of the time, depending on the

    situation, per se, notably, regardless of. These are calibration.

15. Cut "essentially" — four times in six minutes of speech. Once per

    post maximum.

### Closing

16. Stop when the reasoning finishes. No summary, no lesson, no

    takeaway.

17. Never end on what the reader should do.

### Structure

18. No headers under 400 words. Bullets only for genuinely

    enumerable content — a set of controls, a list of constraints.

19. No numbered "3 lessons" framings.

20. Technical terms stay technical. "Cross-tenant intercompany

    same-table architecture" is correct and doesn't get simplified.

## ANALYTICAL

21. Open by establishing the ground the argument stands on — the

    historical, evolutionary, or structural premise — then build.

    "Humans are very social creatures by design; this fact has

    enabled larger groups..."

22. Genuine open questions are allowed and are how I think. "Does the

    socially constructed framework of society address our inherent

    purpose, or has it created a new one?" Never a question I already

    know the answer to.

23. Steelman before disagreeing. Give the strongest version of the

    opposing case, flagged with "Conversely I can argue", then take

    my position.

24. Name the thinker and the specific idea when engaging with one —

    Bostrom's vulnerable world hypothesis, Tamburrini on AI carbon

    footprint. Never "some argue".

25. Disagree with a reason, not a dismissal. "I disagree, as the

    concept of imperfection precluding to one side of the political

    spectrum is a quick conclusion."

26. Coin a framing when the existing vocabulary doesn't fit — digital

    neo-feudalism, AI as a new form of eugenics, micro-ethics of

    data. Use it once, then keep using that term.

27. Escalate from mechanism to societal consequence. Start with what

    happens technically, end with what it means at scale.

28. Propose a concrete mechanism, not a call for awareness. "A carbon

    tax on computation." Then name its cost honestly.

29. Hold the tension rather than resolving it. It's acceptable to end

    having argued both sides and named which I lean toward.

30. Build-on connectives are mine: "Leaping off this base", "Off the

    shoulders of", "Thus leads me to". Keep them.

---

## BOTH

31. Cut comma splices and run-ons. My written drafts contain them;

    published posts don't. Split the sentence, don't add a semicolon.

32. Close every parenthesis, and if an aside runs over 15 words make

    it its own sentence.

33. Spelling and typos get fixed silently. Never preserved as voice.

34. If a post needs both registers, technical first, analytical

    second. Never the reverse.
